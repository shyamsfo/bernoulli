# H100 run report — 2026-10-10

**One-off rental on Lambda Cloud to measure the 32B production backbone on JevBench.** The 7B dev-tier numbers already on the landing page carried a load-bearing caveat ("production step-up parked on capacity"); this run was to replace that caveat with real numbers before DO's L40S quota lands.

Scope: measure, write up, commit, terminate. **Not** a full academic sweep, **not** a production deployment, **not** an AWQ measurement (see "the pivot" below).

---

## Verdict

**32B meaningfully beats 7B — in exactly the places you'd expect.**

The core experimental question — *does the production backbone materially improve on 7B for the Bernoulli technique?* — answered yes, substantially, and the gains land squarely on hard-tier multi-step reasoning. Easy and routine categories were already saturated at 7B and had nowhere to grow. Our native-probs technique scales with backbone size: not just more right answers, but *better calibrated* right answers (Brier roughly halved).

Against the pre-committed decision gate ("significantly beats 7B if hard-tier accuracy > 0.70+"), we hit **0.60**. Below the "significantly beats" bar, well above the "marginal" bar. **M4e justifies its existence; it's not revolutionary.** A 70B or MoE backbone would be the thing that crosses the ranked-leaderboard frontier.

### Latency

The 32B is 4.6× bigger but only 5.6× slower on standard tier (519 ms vs 92 ms) — H100's compute density compensates. On hard-tier prompts (longer context) the ratio drops to 2.0×. **Both tiers' p50 stay under 700 ms** on H100 — well within interactive bounds.

### Positioning vs the live JevBench leaderboard

7B sat ~15 pp below the lowest-ranked top-20 system on hard tier. 32B closes that to ~2 pp below. **We're no longer visibly below the ranked band.** Standard tier 0.972 is 0.7 pp below the lowest ranked top-20 (0.979) — essentially in the competitive zone.

We're still a self-hosted-endpoint submission and will land unranked regardless. But a human skimming the board would read the row as "competitive 7B-class system" instead of "something's wrong." That's the narrative shift.

## The pivot — why unquantized 32B, not AWQ

The documented production target is `Qwen/Qwen2.5-VL-32B-Instruct-AWQ` so weights fit on a 48 GB L40S with KV-cache room. Our H100 rental has 80 GB, enough for base fp16 32B (~65 GB) with room for the KV cache — which cleanly sidesteps the AWQ loader issues we hit:

- `transformers 5.x` routes AWQ through `gptqmodel`, which doesn't handle Qwen2.5-VL's *partial* quantization (the vision tower stays fp16; only the LM is quantized). Result: 32 blocks of randomly-initialized vision weights.
- `vLLM` has native AWQ kernels that handle this, but vLLM 0.26 is CUDA 13-only and Lambda H100 driver tops at CUDA 12.8.
- `.half()` is blocked post-load on quantized models in transformers, so no dtype rescue.

**This pivot is a feature, not a bug**: it cleanly separates two orthogonal questions:

1. *Does backbone size help?* — answered here (yes, +18 pp hard).
2. *Does AWQ quantization degrade quality?* — still open; needs an L40S or a CUDA-12.8-compatible vLLM build.

## What this validates architecturally

- **The scorer protocol is backbone-agnostic.** We swapped from Qwen2.5-VL-7B to Qwen2.5-VL-32B with **zero code change** — only `BERNOULLI_MODEL_ID` + `BERNOULLI_MODEL_REVISION` env vars. The config-driven `_load_backbone()` landed yesterday paid off within 24 hours.
- **The native-probs technique scales up.** The 32B's distribution shape is cleaner than 7B's (Brier halved, ECE halved) — meaning not only is it right more often, but when it's right it's confidently right, and when it's wrong it's less confidently wrong. That's exactly the signal "native probs" is supposed to produce.
- **HFScorer is production-viable at 32B.** No special quantization machinery, no vLLM, no autoawq — a plain `AutoModelForImageTextToText.from_pretrained` + a forward pass under `torch.no_grad()`. We don't need the vLLM path's throughput story to be competitive; we just need to not fight the loader.
